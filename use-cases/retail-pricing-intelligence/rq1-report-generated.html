Executive Summary

Card savings get computed to the cent. Benefit savings get a checkbox.

Every storefront in this study puts a precise dollar figure on a card-linked offer before the shopper reaches checkout. Amazon's cart states the post-promotion price outright. Not one of them does the equivalent for a benefit tender. SNAP, HSA/FSA and OTC appear as eligibility flags — a badge, a filter, a help article — and never as an amount. The shopper learns that an item can be bought with a benefit, never what they would pay if they did.

Costco states the constraint in its own words: "Costco cannot identify specific item eligibility prior to a transaction being tendered." That is the industry position rendered explicit — eligibility is resolved at the register, not in the aisle.

Method & Scope

Four storefronts, four agents, every claim traceable to a session replay.

One Browserbase agent per storefront navigated the live site as an unauthenticated shopper — homepage, a grocery search, a health/OTC search, product pages, and the cart — without logging in, checking out, or submitting payment. Each returned a structured findings object plus a prose narrative. Costco alone took 84 steps over 13.7 minutes; Instacart took 155 steps over 11.0 minutes.

Two limits are worth stating plainly. First, Retailer is not scored in this pass. No agent run completed against Retailer.com, so every Retailer cell below is marked n/a rather than estimated — the client's own column will be filled when that run lands. Second, Amazon and Instacart both failed on the first attempt against bot protection and completed only on a retry pass; the findings reported here are from the successful runs.

Instacart was studied through the Costco storefront on Instacart, so its findings describe the Instacart payment layer rather than Costco's own. Allbirds stands in as a representative Shopify merchant to characterise the Shop Pay stack, not as a competitor in its own right.

The Core Finding

Card savings are computed. Benefit savings are a checkbox.

The asymmetry is consistent across every storefront studied. Where a card is involved, the retailer does the arithmetic for the shopper and shows the result in dollars. Where a benefit is involved, the retailer confirms eligibility and stops.

How card savings look today

"Pay $15.29 $0.00 for this order. Get a $50 Amazon Gift Card upon approval for Amazon Visa."

Exact dollars, computed in-cart, before checkout. (Amazon)

PROOF · Amazon guest cart, subtotal $15.29 — the Visa offer is resolved to a dollar figure on the page

How benefit savings look today

"FSA or HSA eligible item… If you have a card, you can add it in checkout."

"Costco cannot identify specific item eligibility prior to a transaction being tendered."

Eligibility only. No amount, no balance, no deduction — resolved at the register. (Amazon, Costco)

PROOF · Costco's accepted-methods page: FSA/HSA restricted to "Pharmacies, Optical and Hearing Aid Departments"

The gap is the opportunity.

A shopper using a benefit tender is, almost by definition, price-sensitive. They are the population least served by "eligible" and most served by "you will pay $0.00 out of pocket." No retailer in this study has claimed that ground.

InstacartDiscovery effort: Moderate · 155 steps · 11.0 min
Benefit discovery
The widest tender set in the study and the most visible EBT treatment: a "SNAP Eligible" filter chip on search and an "Accepts EBT" badge on store cards. FSA/HSA and OTC Network are documented only in Help Center articles and a landing page — no on-product badges appeared in the storefront browsed.
Savings by tender
Promotions are quantified — Klarna $25 off a first order, Venmo $5 off $15+ — as are fee thresholds ("Add $4.96 to get $0 delivery fee", "$10 Min. to checkout"). Benefit tenders carry no equivalent figure.
Constraint
Benefits do not stack: "You can't place an order using both an FSA/HSA card and an OTC Network card/EBT SNAP." EBT also requires a card on file to cover fees, taxes and tips.
Notable quotes

"Order FSA/HSA-eligible items right to your door. You can add non-eligible items to your cart too."

CostcoDiscovery effort: Moderate-to-high · 84 steps · 13.7 min
Benefit discovery
Benefit acceptance is real but channel-restricted and easy to misread. FSA/HSA and Medicare Advantage OTC cards are promoted as accepted "at every register" — in warehouse. The accepted-methods page limits FSA/HSA to "Pharmacies, Optical and Hearing Aid Departments". EBT is warehouse-only. None is a Costco.com checkout option.
Eligibility cues
An "FSA Eligible" badge exists and returns 224 results — but only if the shopper searches the literal phrase "fsa eligible". There is no filter checkbox for FSA/HSA/EBT/OTC in the sidebar, and no badge surfaces through ordinary category browsing.
Savings by tender
Not surfaced at the point of shopping. Citi cash-back tiers appear only once an item is in the cart; the non-member 5% surcharge lives on a secondary FAQ. No lowest-cost-by-tender comparison exists anywhere.
Notable quotes

"Costco cannot identify specific item eligibility prior to a transaction being tendered."

"Costco now accepts Medicare Advantage OTC Benefits, FSA and HSA cards at every register."

"Don't let your funds go to waste! Shop with us to stretch your FSA, HSA, and Medicare Advantage Prepaid Benefits Cards before they expire."

Shopify (Allbirds)Discovery effort: High · 43 steps · 4.9 min
Benefit discovery
None observed, of any kind. A DTC footwear merchant sells no benefit-eligible goods, so the absence is structural — it is included here to characterise the Shopify stack against benefit-accepting grocery and pharmacy retail.
Wallets
The one clear win in the study. The cart drawer presents four express buttons side by side — Amazon Pay, G Pay, PayPal, Shop Pay — and the hosted checkout repeats an Express checkout row above the contact form.

Best Practices Observed

Patterns worth borrowing.

Amazon
Resolve the offer to a price, in the cart

Stating "Pay $0.00 for this order" removes all arithmetic from the shopper. The mechanic is proven — it is simply never pointed at a benefit tender.

Instacart
Badge eligibility at the store level, before entry

An "Accepts EBT" chip on the store card tells a shopper whether a trip is worth starting, rather than making them discover it at checkout.

Instacart
Make eligibility a first-class search filter

A "SNAP Eligible" chip alongside Brand and Dietary treats benefit eligibility as a normal shopping facet, not a payments edge case.

Shopify
Present every wallet as one express row

Four tenders offered side by side in the cart drawer, repeated at checkout. Zero hunting, zero hidden options.

Amazon
Give benefits a destination, not just a badge

A dedicated FSA | HSA store and a SNAP hub give benefit shoppers somewhere to start, with "No separate FSA or HSA order required" removing a known friction.

Costco
Name the expiry pressure

"Don't let your funds go to waste… before they expire" is the sharpest benefit-shopper motivation found anywhere in the study — buried on a pharmacy microsite.

Opportunities for Retailer

Where the industry leaves room to lead.

Compute the out-of-pocket figure for benefit tenders

No retailer studied does this. Amazon proves the cart can resolve an offer to a price; nobody has pointed that mechanic at SNAP, HSA/FSA or OTC. "You will pay $4.12 out of pocket" is an unclaimed position, and the shoppers it serves are the most price-sensitive in the market.

Show the benefit balance before the cart, not at the register

Not one storefront surfaced a benefit balance pre-checkout. Costco concedes it "cannot identify specific item eligibility prior to a transaction being tendered." A visible remaining balance while shopping would be a category first.

Make eligibility a filter, not a keyword

Costco's FSA badge exists on 224 items but requires searching the exact phrase "fsa eligible" — invisible to ordinary browsing. Instacart's filter chip is the better pattern and the cheaper one to match.

Do not let acceptance breadth hide behind channel rules

Costco accepts FSA/HSA and OTC widely, yet a shopper reading "at every register" cannot tell it excludes Costco.com. Where Retailer accepts a tender online, saying so unambiguously at the point of shopping is itself a differentiator.

Audit advertised versus actually offered

Allbirds advertises Afterpay on a live page that the checkout does not honour. A recurring agent sweep of advertised-versus-offered tenders is cheap to run and catches this class of drift before customers do.
