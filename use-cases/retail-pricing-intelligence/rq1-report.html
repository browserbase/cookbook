Executive Summary

Card savings get spelled out before checkout. Benefit savings don't.

Merchant A's payments team asked us to compare how the largest online retailers handle the payment experience end to end, from the moment a shopper starts figuring out how to pay, through checkout, to refunds. This is the first phase of that study, and it looks at one stage: payment discovery, meaning everything a shopper can learn about their payment and benefit options before they reach the checkout page. Can they tell which cards and wallets are accepted? Do they know whether an item can be bought with SNAP, an HSA/FSA, or an OTC benefit card? And can they see what they'll actually pay?

We ran the same review on five retailers on their live sites (Retailer, Marketplace A, Marketplace B, Merchant C, and a representative Commerce Platform D store) and saved a screenshot of every claim in this report.

One pattern stood out. All five make card-linked savings easy to see in real dollars up front: Marketplace A's cart even does the math for you (“Cost after savings: $0.00”) the moment you add its card. None of them do the same for benefits. Pay with SNAP, an HSA, an FSA, or an OTC card and the site will tell you an item is eligible, but never what you'll actually pay, and never your remaining balance. So the shoppers who most need to know their out-of-pocket cost get the least help working it out.

What to carry into planning

Benefit discovery is a gap across the whole category. No one has built it. Retailer is already ahead on the hard part: it accepts more benefit tenders online than anyone here (EBT, HSA/FSA, and OTC), and it owns its own payment rail in OnePay. That puts Retailer in the best position of the group to be first to show shoppers what they'll really pay with their benefits.

The Core Finding

Card savings are computed. Benefit savings are a checkbox.

How card savings look today

“Get $50 off: Pay $18.60 $0.00 upon approval for Marketplace A Visa.”

“Current subtotal: $18.60 / Gift Card savings: −$50.00 / Cost after savings: $0.00 / Savings remaining: $31.40.”

Exact dollars, computed in-cart, before checkout. (Marketplace A)

PROOF · Marketplace A guest cart: Visa offer quantified in-cart

How benefit savings look today

“FSA or HSA eligible item… If you have a card, you can add it in checkout.”

“You must also add a credit or debit card… to use your EBT card.”

Eligibility only. No amount. No balance. Deferred to checkout. (Marketplace A, Marketplace B)

PROOF · Marketplace A PDP: “FSA or HSA eligible” badge, no dollar amount

It's the same story at all five retailers, Retailer included: the SNAP, HSA/FSA, or OTC shopper, the person who most needs to know what they'll actually pay, gets the least help working it out. Because everyone does it this way, this isn't one company slipping up. It's just how the category works right now, which is exactly why there's room to do it better.

Best Practices Observed

Patterns worth borrowing.

Commerce Platform D
Express-checkout row

Four one-click wallets surfaced at the cart, before any form, the clearest path-to-pay of any player.

Marketplace A
Dedicated benefit storefronts + filter

A standalone FSA/HSA store and an EBT hub, plus a real search filter, make eligibility browsable rather than hidden.

Marketplace B
Eligibility at the shelf

A “SNAP Eligible” filter chip and “Accepts EBT” store badges tell benefit shoppers where they can shop before they invest time.

Merchant C
Benefit-urgency messaging

“Stretch your benefits before they expire”, a rare example of proactively nudging benefit utilization (albeit warehouse-only).

Opportunities for Retailer

Where the industry leaves room to lead.

Quantify benefit savings pre-checkout. No competitor shows “you'll pay $X out of pocket with your HSA/EBT.” Retailer could compute it in-cart the way Marketplace A computes card savings today.
Ship a benefit balance tracker. Zero of four surface a running EBT/HSA/FSA/OTC balance. A live tracker turns a compliance feature into a loyalty driver.
Unify benefit eligibility into one badge + filter. Today eligibility is fragmented (Marketplace A labels, Marketplace B chips, Merchant C keyword-only). One consistent “benefit eligible” badge and filter beats all of them.
Bring benefits online where rivals keep them in-store. Merchant C confines OTC/FSA/HSA to the warehouse. Merchant A's online EBT leadership is a head start to extend across all benefit tenders online.
Match express-checkout velocity. Commerce Platform D's four-wallet express row sets the discovery bar for one-click pay; parity here protects conversion.
